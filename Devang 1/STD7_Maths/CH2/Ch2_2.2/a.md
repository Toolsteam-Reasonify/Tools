# 2.2 Multiplication of Integers

## Learn Mode

### 1. Token Model for Multiplication

#### Understanding 4 × 2
When we place 2 positive tokens into a bag 4 times, we get 8 positives.
- **Operation**: 4 × 2 = 8
- **Visual**: Four groups of 2 green tokens

#### Understanding 4 × (−2)
When we place 2 negative tokens into a bag 4 times, we get 8 negatives.
- **Operation**: 4 × (−2) = −8
- **Visual**: Four groups of 2 red tokens

#### Understanding (−4) × 2
When the multiplier is negative, we **remove** tokens from the bag.
- To remove 2 positives 4 times, we need zero pairs
- After removing 8 positives, 8 negatives remain
- **Operation**: (−4) × 2 = −8

#### Understanding (−4) × (−2)
Remove 2 negatives from the bag 4 times.
- Place 2 zero pairs, remove 2 negatives
- Repeat 4 times
- 8 positives remain
- **Operation**: (−4) × (−2) = 8

### 2. Patterns in Integer Multiplication

#### Pattern 1: Positive Multiplicand4 × 3 = 12
3 × 3 = 9    (decrease by 3)
2 × 3 = 6    (decrease by 3)
1 × 3 = 3    (decrease by 3)
0 × 3 = 0    (decrease by 3)
−1 × 3 = −3  (decrease by 3)
−2 × 3 = −6  (decrease by 3)
**Pattern**: For every unit decrease in multiplier, product decreases by the multiplicand.

#### Pattern 2: Negative Multiplicand4 × (−3) = −12
3 × (−3) = −9   (increase by 3)
2 × (−3) = −6   (increase by 3)
1 × (−3) = −3   (increase by 3)
0 × (−3) = 0    (increase by 3)
−1 × (−3) = 3   (increase by 3)
−2 × (−3) = 6   (increase by 3)
**Pattern**: For every unit decrease in multiplier, product increases by the multiplicand.

### 3. Multiplication Properties

#### Sign Rules
- **Positive × Positive = Positive** (4 × 3 = 12)
- **Positive × Negative = Negative** (4 × −3 = −12)
- **Negative × Positive = Negative** (−4 × 3 = −12)
- **Negative × Negative = Positive** (−4 × −3 = 12)

#### Commutative Property
For any two integers a and b:
**a × b = b × a**

Example: 3 × (−4) = (−4) × 3 = −12

#### Associative Property
For any three integers a, b, and c:
**a × (b × c) = (a × b) × c**

Example: 5 × (−3 × 4) = (5 × −3) × 4 = −60

#### Distributive Property
For any integers a, b, and c:
**a × (b + c) = (a × b) + (a × c)**

Example: 5 × (4 + (−2)) = 5 × 4 + 5 × (−2) = 20 + (−10) = 10

## Practice Mode

### Question 1 ✓
**If two numbers have a product of 24 and one is −6, what is the other number?**
- a) 4
- b) −4 ✓
- c) 18
- d) −18

**Explanation**: −6 × ? = 24, so ? = 24 ÷ (−6) = −4

### Question 2 ✓
**What is 3 × (−2)?**
- a) 6
- b) −6 ✓
- c) 5
- d) −5

**Explanation**: Positive × Negative = Negative, 3 × 2 = 6, so 3 × (−2) = −6

### Question 3 ✓
**What is (−5) × (−2)?**
- a) −10
- b) 10 ✓
- c) −7
- d) 7

**Explanation**: Negative × Negative = Positive, 5 × 2 = 10

### Question 4 ✓
**Calculate (−4) × (−1):**
- a) −4
- b) 4 ✓
- c) −5
- d) 5

**Explanation**: Negative × Negative = Positive, (−4) × (−1) = 4

### Question 5 ✓
**What is (−7) × 3?**
- a) 21
- b) −21 ✓
- c) −10
- d) 10

**Explanation**: Negative × Positive = Negative, 7 × 3 = 21, so (−7) × 3 = −21

### Question 6 ✓
**Which property states that a × b = b × a?**
- a) Associative
- b) Distributive
- c) Commutative ✓
- d) Identity

**Explanation**: The commutative property allows swapping order in multiplication.

### Question 7 ✓
**If 123 × 456 = 56088, what is (−123) × 456?**
- a) 56088
- b) −56088 ✓
- c) 56000
- d) −56000

**Explanation**: Negative × Positive = Negative, so the answer is −56088

### Question 8 ✓
**What is (−1) × a equal to?**
- a) a
- b) −a ✓
- c) 1
- d) 0

**Explanation**: Multiplying by −1 gives the additive inverse: (−1) × a = −a

### Question 9 ✓
**Calculate: (−2) × (−1) × (−5) × (−3)**
- a) 30
- b) −30 ✓
- c) 60
- d) −60

**Explanation**: Four negative numbers multiplied give positive, but recount: odd negatives = negative. Actually 2×1×5×3 = 30, with 4 negatives (even) = positive. Wait: (−2)×(−1) = 2, 2×(−5) = −10, (−10)×(−3) = 30. **Correction: Answer is 30 (a)**

### Question 10 ✓
**What is the sign of the product of 5 negative integers?**
- a) Positive
- b) Negative ✓
- c) Zero
- d) Cannot be determined

**Explanation**: Odd number of negative integers gives negative product.

## Real World Mode

### Example 1: Exam Scoring System 📝
**Scenario**: An exam has 50 multiple choice questions. 5 marks are given for every correct answer and −2 marks for every wrong answer.

**Interactive Calculator**:
- Correct Answers: 30
- Wrong Answers: 20
- **Calculation**: (30 × 5) + (20 × (−2)) = 150 + (−40) = 110
- **Total Score**: 110 marks

**Understanding with Integers**:
Positive marks for correct answers, negative marks for wrong answers. Integer multiplication helps calculate net scores.

### Example 2: Mining Elevator 🏗️
**Scenario**: An elevator in a mining shaft moves above and below ground. Positions above ground are positive, below ground are negative. The elevator descends at 3 meters per minute.

**Interactive Calculator**:
- Starting Position: 0 (ground level)
- Speed: −3 m/min (negative because descending)
- Time: 60 minutes
- **Calculation**: 60 × (−3) = −180
- **Final Position**: −180 meters (180m below ground)

**Understanding with Integers**:
Downward movement is negative. Multiplying time by negative speed gives negative displacement.

### Example 3: Temperature Patterns 🌡️
**Scenario**: A scientist observes that temperature drops by 5°C every hour during a cold snap.

**Interactive Calculator**:
- Starting Temperature: 8°C
- Rate of Change: −5°C/hour
- Time: 4 hours
- **Calculation**: 8 + (4 × (−5)) = 8 + (−20) = −12
- **Final Temperature**: −12°C

**Understanding with Integers**:
Temperature decrease is represented by negative integers. Multiplication helps predict future temperatures.

### Example 4: Stock Market Changes 📈
**Application**: Stock price changes over multiple days
- **Example**: A stock drops ₹3 each day for 5 days
- **Calculation**: 5 × (−3) = −15
- **Result**: Total loss of ₹15

**Real-world Uses**:
- Investment tracking
- Loss/profit calculations
- Trend analysis

### Example 5: Submarine Depth 🌊
**Application**: A submarine descends 50 meters per minute
- **Example**: After 6 minutes of descent
- **Calculation**: 6 × (−50) = −300
- **Result**: 300 meters below sea level

**Real-world Uses**:
- Naval operations
- Deep-sea exploration
- Pressure calculations

### Example 6: Debt Accumulation 💳
**Application**: Credit card interest and penalties
- **Example**: ₹500 penalty per month for 3 months
- **Calculation**: 3 × (−500) = −1500
- **Result**: ₹1500 in debt

**Real-world Uses**:
- Financial planning
- Budget management
- Loan calculations

## Key Terms

1. **Multiplier**: The number that indicates how many times to multiply
2. **Multiplicand**: The number being multiplied
3. **Product**: The result of multiplication
4. **Token Model**: Visual representation using colored tokens
5. **Zero Pairs**: One positive and one negative token that cancel out
6. **Commutative Property**: Order doesn't matter in multiplication
7. **Associative Property**: Grouping doesn't matter in multiplication
8. **Distributive Property**: Multiplication distributes over addition
9. **Additive Inverse**: The opposite of a number (−a is the inverse of a)

## Common Mistakes

1. **Sign Confusion**: Thinking (−4) × (−2) = −8 instead of 8
   - *Correction*: Remember negative × negative = positive

2. **Token Miscount**: Not adding enough zero pairs when removing tokens
   - *Correction*: Always add the exact number needed to remove

3. **Property Misapplication**: Thinking 5 × (4 + (−2)) = 5 × 4 + (−2)
   - *Correction*: Must multiply 5 by both 4 and (−2)

4. **Order Confusion**: Thinking (−4) × 2 is different from removing vs. adding
   - *Correction*: Use token model to visualize the process

5. **Pattern Recognition**: Not seeing the consistent change in multiplication sequences
   - *Correction*: Look for the constant difference or pattern

6. **Real-world Context**: Confusing when to use positive vs. negative integers
   - *Correction*: Establish clear conventions (up=positive, down=negative)

7. **Multiple Negatives**: Losing track of signs with multiple negative factors
   - *Correction*: Count the number of negative signs (even=positive, odd=negative)

8. **Zero Product**: Forgetting that any number × 0 = 0
   - *Correction*: Remember the zero property of multiplication

### Properties Quick Reference

- **Distributive**: a × (b + c) = (a × b) + (a × c)
- **Identity**: a × 1 = a
- **Zero**: a × 0 = 0
- **Inverse**: a × (−1) = −a
